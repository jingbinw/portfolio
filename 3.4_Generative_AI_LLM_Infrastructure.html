Context

Why this topic matters

Modern language models are often discussed in terms of parameters or model capability alone. This project examines the larger production system required to prepare data, train a model, evaluate it, and serve it to users.

Challenge

Comparing incomplete disclosures

Public cost and resource claims use different hardware, accounting boundaries, and definitions of “training cost.” A GPU-hour is accelerator time—not a universal price tag—and reported figures do not reveal every cost.

Approach & solution

Make the lifecycle and caveats visible

I organized the subject into a seven-stage pipeline and a data-to-deployment resource stack, then compared GPT-4, Llama 3.1 405B, and DeepSeek-V3 using clearly labeled reported facts and disclosure limits.

Project outcome: A practical framework for explaining how pretraining, post-training, infrastructure, energy, evaluation, and deployment fit together—and why model cost claims need a clearly defined scope.

01 · Lifecycle

The LLM training pipeline

A foundation model is built in stages; each stage changes what the system knows, how it behaves, and whether it is ready to serve.

  1. 1Collect
  2. 2Clean
  3. 3Tokenize
  4. 4Pretrain
  5. 5Post-train
  6. 6Evaluate
  7. 7Deploy
1

Collect & license

Web, books, code, documents, and partner data; define rights, privacy, and provenance.

2

Clean & prepare

Filter spam and unsafe material; deduplicate, balance domains and languages, and remove sensitive data.

3

Tokenize

Convert text into tokens, pack sequences, and build training batches.

4

Pretrain

A Transformer predicts the next token; backpropagation updates billions of parameters.

5

Post-train

Supervised examples plus preference optimization or RLHF shape instruction following and helpfulness.

6

Evaluate & red-team

Benchmark quality, bias, robustness, privacy, and misuse; iterate or block release.

7

Deploy & monitor

Serve on GPUs or other accelerators; optimize latency and cost; monitor abuse, drift, and failures.

Key distinction: Pretraining builds broad statistical capability; post-training steers that capability into an assistant. Deployment is a separate, ongoing engineering and operations problem.

02 · Learning loop

What happens during pretraining?

The model is not storing a library of answers. It learns statistical patterns in token sequences by repeatedly predicting what comes next.

Input tokens “The cat sat on the …” Tokenizer maps text into token IDs; context is represented as vectors.
Transformer Attention and layers process each token in context; parameters encode learned patterns.
Prediction A probability distribution is produced for the next token, such as “mat” or “floor.”
Compare with target Compare the prediction with the real next token and compute prediction error (loss).
Update model weights Backpropagation and an optimizer update weights; repeat at scale.

Billions of training examples × many optimization steps → a model that generalizes beyond the exact sentences it saw.

03 · Infrastructure

The resource stack: more than GPUs

Quality and cost depend on the interaction between data, hardware, people, software, energy, and elapsed time.

Data

Quality & provenance

High-quality, diverse, legally usable corpora; deduplication, filtering, annotation, and synthetic examples.

Compute

Distributed hardware

Thousands of accelerators, high-bandwidth networking, fast storage, and distributed training software.

Energy & facilities

Power and cooling

Electricity for chips plus cooling, networking, and power conversion; carbon intensity varies by grid.

Time & reliability

Long-running jobs

Large runs last weeks or months; failures, checkpointing, evaluation, and restarts add overhead.

People & operations

Cross-functional teams

Research, data engineering, infrastructure, safety, evaluation, security, and product teams.

Total cost extends beyond the final run: R&D experiments, data acquisition, staff, infrastructure, safety work, and inference all matter.

04 · Published examples

Three models, three disclosure stories

Reported figures are not perfectly comparable: hardware, accounting boundaries, and what counts as “training cost” differ.

Limited technical disclosure

GPT-4 · OpenAI

What is known: Transformer; pretrained on public and licensed data; post-trained with RLHF.

What is not public: Model size, hardware, training compute, dataset construction, and training cost.

Interpretation: A high-profile example of limited technical disclosure.

Reported compute & emissions

Llama 3.1 405B · Meta

Data: Over 15 trillion tokens; 405B parameters.

Compute: 30.84 million H100 GPU-hours; trained using more than 16,000 H100 GPUs.

Energy / emissions: Meta reports 8,930 tCO₂e location-based emissions for the 405B run.

Reported compute & cost estimate

DeepSeek-V3 · DeepSeek

Data: 14.8 trillion tokens; 671B total / 37B active parameters.

Compute & time: 2.788 million H800 GPU-hours; pretraining took less than two months on 2,048 GPUs.

Cost estimate: $5.576M at an assumed $2/H800-hour; excludes prior R&D and ablations.

The dollar figure is an accelerator-rental estimate for the official run—not the full cost to invent, build, and operate the model.

05 · Interpreting the numbers

Why training cost is hard to compare

A GPU-hour is a unit of accelerator time—not a universal price tag. The true bill depends on chip, utilization, cloud versus owned hardware, and scope.

Training compute cost ≈ accelerator-hours × effective $/hour

Then add: data + storage/network + power/cooling + staff + experiments + evaluation + safety + operations.

06 · Key takeaways

A model is a sociotechnical production system

A strong model is the outcome of more than a large neural network.

  • Data quality, diversity, and provenance shape what a model can learn.
  • Pretraining builds broad capability; post-training shapes assistant behavior.
  • Compute, networking, storage, and reliability determine whether training can scale.
  • Energy and emissions should be reported with clear boundaries and methodology.
  • Public cost claims need scope: the final run is not total R&D or lifetime operating cost.
  • Evaluation, safety testing, and monitoring continue after deployment.
