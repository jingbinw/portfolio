Architecture brief · Scenario C

Organizational knowledge assistant for Aegis Financial Global

A proposed permission-aware retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) service for finding and explaining approved internal policies.

Fictional organization

Aegis Financial Global

A multinational financial-services and asset-management organization with approximately 3,500 employees.

Problem to solve

Fragmented, aging policy content

HR, benefits, compliance, IT security, and operating policies span SharePoint, Confluence, and legacy drives. Some documents are role-restricted.

Scenario indicators

Search is costly and uncertain

Scenario figures indicate more than 40% of source material is outdated or superseded and employees spend an estimated 4.5 hours per week searching or waiting for answers.

Decision status: CONDITIONAL — do not deploy with confidential or protected records yet. Continue architecture design and a restricted proof of concept using synthetic data or explicitly approved non-sensitive sources. Protected content remains blocked until all required gates pass.
Recommended approach

Managed, permission-aware RAG

Use an approved managed enterprise cloud, corporate identity provider, governed hybrid search/vector index, and enterprise LLM API under reviewed contractual terms. The model synthesizes retrieved evidence; it is not the authoritative policy store. No fine-tuning or training from scratch is proposed.

Pilot boundary

Non-sensitive content only

Consider a time-limited, restricted pilot only after security, privacy, procurement, legal, and architecture reviews. If approvals or access controls cannot be verified, retain permission-filtered enterprise search and human support.

Architecture & data flow

Five stages, with authorization before generation

Only approved sources are indexed. Each request is filtered for the authenticated user's document permissions before authorized passages can reach the model API.

Conceptual target architecture—not a deployed Aegis system. Dashed arrows show identity checks, human escalation, and the monitored feedback/re-index loop.

Critical design rule: Document-level authorization happens in retrieval before any passage is sent to the model. A prompt instruction is not an access-control mechanism.

Hosting, controls & accountability

Operating design for a restricted pilot

Hosting & dependencies

  • Use a managed enterprise cloud tenant selected through approved cloud and vendor processes.
  • Keep repositories, index, application services, secrets, logs, and network paths inside the approved security boundary and permitted regions.
  • Use managed app/API hosting, search/vector storage, key/secrets service, centralized logging/SIEM, and a contracted enterprise LLM API.
  • Keep endpoints private or equivalently restricted; do not expose unauthenticated public endpoints.
  • Choose provider, model, region, and service tier only after review; none are assumed approved.

Identity & data protection

  • Corporate SSO/MFA, authoritative role/group entitlements, least-privilege service identities.
  • Separate development, test, and production; use managed secrets and credential rotation.
  • Re-check access per request and after group, role, termination, or document-permission changes.
  • Scope caches and conversation history per user; invalidate restricted content after revocation.
  • Encrypt in transit and at rest; minimize prompt/passage retention and keep logs metadata-focused.

Content governance & freshness

  • Document owners confirm canonical status, classification, owner, version, approval, and effective date.
  • Quarantine superseded content; ingestion alone does not make a document authoritative.
  • Proposed pilot target: daily incremental indexing plus event-driven updates for critical policy changes.
  • Invalidate stale content and revoked access; maintain controlled re-indexing.
  • Use synthetic data or explicitly approved non-sensitive sources until reviews pass.

Named ownership

  • Document owners: approve sources and metadata.
  • Data/AI Engineering: prepare and index content.
  • IAM/Security: enforce authorization and monitor access.
  • AI platform/service owner: manage endpoint, prompts, reliability, and operations.
  • HR/Legal/Compliance: handle sensitive, conflicting, or high-impact questions.
  • IT Operations/Security: service health, incidents, on-call, and rollback.

Negotiate no-training use of organizational inputs where available, minimal provider-side retention, deletion/return commitments, and approved subprocessors and data locations. Privacy, Legal, Records Management, and Security must approve retention and contract terms before launch.

Options & trade-offs

Why permission-aware RAG—and what it costs

RAG is a preferred target after approvals and tests, not an automatic deployment decision. It does not eliminate hallucination or stale-source risk.

Risk, trade-offs & monitoring

Controls must be observable and actionable

Each risk has a preventive control, a signal to monitor, and a response path. Tune acceptance thresholds with accountable owners before a pilot; no thresholds are assumed approved here.

Unauthorized disclosure or IAM bypass

Control: Pre-retrieval document permissions, least privilege, per-request checks, per-user cache isolation, revocation tests.

Monitor / respond: Authorization decisions, denied access, anomalous retrieval, and access incidents. Immediately disable generation after a confirmed leak or bypass.

Stale, ownerless, or conflicting content

Control: Named source owners, canonical versions, effective dates, superseded-file quarantine, scheduled and event-driven re-indexing.

Monitor / respond: Source age, version freshness, missing owner/approval metadata, conflicting results. Pause ingestion if ownership or authoritative status cannot be established.

Unsupported answer or misleading citation

Control: Ground answers in authorized passages, cite source/version/date, abstain on missing or conflicting evidence, human escalation for sensitive questions.

Monitor / respond: Groundedness, citation accuracy, unsupported-answer rate, abstention quality, user feedback, and weekly quality sampling. Roll back to search-only if approved thresholds are exceeded.

Prompt injection or content misuse

Control: Treat retrieved text and prompts as untrusted input; never let content instructions override authorization or policy; conduct prompt-injection testing.

Monitor / respond: Red-team results, suspicious access patterns, reported failures, unresolved vulnerabilities. Pause expansion after a serious incident or failed security test.

Vendor, privacy, residency, and retention failure

Control: Vendor due diligence, approved regions/subprocessors, DPA and security terms, no-training terms where available, retention/deletion review.

Monitor / respond: Contract/approval status, provider changes, data-location and retention evidence. Pause if terms or required approvals lapse or cannot be verified.

Cost, latency, availability, and support burden

Control: Small pilot, quotas, budget alerts, measured capacity sizing, named service owner, on-call and tested disable/rollback switch.

Monitor / respond: Availability, latency, errors, retries, token/cost per request and successful grounded answer. Reassess or retire if costs exceed approved value or operations cannot support it.

Operational telemetry

Approved actor ID or pseudonym, timestamp, request ID, retrieved document IDs/versions, authorization result, model/API version, latency, token/cost usage, citation references, abstention/escalation, and administrative changes.

Protect logs and send them to approved SIEM/monitoring. Do not retain full sensitive prompts or passages unless explicitly approved.

Review cadence & incident readiness

Weekly quality sampling; monthly cost review; ongoing service-health and access monitoring. Assign on-call ownership, severity levels, evidence preservation, escalation/notification paths, vendor-notification duties, and a tested rollback/disable switch.

System boundaries & human oversight

Informational assistance—not decision automation

The system may

  • Search approved internal policy sources the authenticated user is authorized to access.
  • Summarize retrieved passages and provide source title, section, version/effective date, and links.
  • Identify missing, stale, contradictory, or low-confidence evidence and abstain or route the question.
  • Help employees find the correct HR, IT, Compliance, or policy owner.
  • Collect limited, approved feedback for quality monitoring and retrieval improvement.

The system must not

  • Make legal, compliance, investment, credit, employment, or consequential benefits-eligibility decisions.
  • Approve exceptions, waive controls, or take actions for HR, Legal, Compliance, or management.
  • Reveal restricted content or sensitive metadata to unauthorized users.
  • Present unapproved, superseded, or unverified sources as authoritative.
  • Train a vendor model on Aegis content without separate review, explicit authorization, and contract approval.

HR, Legal, Compliance, and document owners remain accountable for policy interpretation, exceptions, conflicting rules, and high-impact questions. Employees should follow cited official procedures or contact the responsible team when uncertain.

Approvals & implementation gates

Evidence required before expanding the pilot

Technical feasibility is not legal permission. These are proposed acceptance gates, not approvals already obtained.

  1. Source readiness — named owners, canonical versions, approval/effective-date metadata, and superseded files quarantined. Decision owners: Business document owners / Records Management
  2. Architecture & vendor — approved cloud region, model/API, vendor assessment, data-flow map, contract, and DPA. Decision owners: Enterprise Architecture / Procurement / Security / Legal
  3. Privacy & legal — data classification, permitted purpose, privacy impact assessment, cross-border and retention review. Decision owners: Privacy Officer / Legal / Compliance
  4. Identity & security — SSO/MFA, least privilege, pre-retrieval authorization, revocation/cache tests, penetration and prompt-injection tests. Decision owners: IAM / CISO / Security Engineering
  5. Quality & operations — held-out evaluation, citation/freshness thresholds, monitoring, support/on-call, incident and rollback drills. Decision owners: AI Service Owner / Business Owners / IT Operations
  6. Pilot authorization — written sign-off, restricted user group, non-sensitive scope, user notice, metrics, review date, and kill switch. Decision owners: AI Governance / Executive Sponsor

Before protected, personal, confidential, regulated, employee, client, or investment-related records are processed, document classification and purpose, privacy and legal review, vendor/subprocessor due diligence, contract terms, AI governance, records/e-discovery, and cross-border/data-residency approvals. Until then, use synthetic data or explicitly approved non-sensitive documents in an isolated test environment—not a public or unreviewed AI service.

Round 4 decision record · Proposed

Proceed in stages; keep a safe fallback

Recommended next step

Restricted proof of concept

Continue architecture design using synthetic data or explicitly approved non-sensitive sources. Keep the user group restricted, document the use purpose, define metrics and review date, and rehearse pause/rollback.

Do not expand until

Approvals and tests are evidenced

Do not connect confidential employee, client, legal, compliance, or security repositories until required approvals and independent security tests pass and representative accuracy, freshness, citation, and role-based access tests meet approved thresholds.

Fallback: If RAG controls cannot be proven, required terms/approvals are missing, or quality and monitoring cannot be supported, retain permission-filtered enterprise search and human support instead of deploying generated answers.

Pause, rollback, or retire when

  • Any confirmed unauthorized disclosure, IAM bypass, or exposure of restricted content/metadata occurs: immediately disable generation.
  • Unsupported answers, materially incorrect citations, or stale-source retrieval exceed approved thresholds: roll back to search-only, especially for high-risk topics.
  • More than half of the relevant corpus is ownerless, contradictory, or unreliable, or a source lacks owner/approval/version/effective date: pause ingestion.
  • Vendor terms, data residency, retention, security assurances, or legal/privacy approvals lapse; a serious incident, unresolved vulnerability, failed test, or ineffective revocation occurs: pause expansion.
  • Critical failures persist, the vendor cannot meet requirements, operating cost exceeds approved value, or a safer approved alternative emerges: retire or replace the service.
